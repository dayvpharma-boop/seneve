Month 1 sets the foundations: thirty short readings, one per day, and a weekly challenge. Days 7, 14, 21 and 28 review the week; days 29 and 30 prepare what comes next.

## Day 1 — Start with what is possible

**Aim:** Choose a realistic first step.

Begin with the day ahead of you. The chapter on faithfulness invites you to care for everyday responsibilities. Choose one appointment you can realistically keep today, while keeping the other suggested activities in perspective. Tracking helps you describe your actions; it measures neither your faith nor your worth.

**For reflection:** Which appointment would you like to prepare for first?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 6; Habits & Challenges specification, sections 3–5 and 10–13.

## Day 2 — Make room for what matters

**Aim:** Review your everyday priorities.

Your priorities become visible in how you use your time. Before filling your day, consider the space you want to give to prayer, reading and your responsibilities. You can adjust the suggested times to your circumstances. A better balance begins with one practical decision within your reach.

**For reflection:** What adjustment would bring your day closer to your priorities?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 1; Habits & Challenges specification, sections 3–5 and 10–13.

## Day 3 — Prepare for your prayer time

**Aim:** Make personal prayer easier to begin.

The book presents prayer as a relationship that accompanies everyday decisions. Prepare a place or a moment where you can pray without rushing. The suggested prayer can support this time; you can also pray in your own words. Afterwards, record what you actually experienced, without comparing yourself with others.

**For reflection:** What helps you settle calmly into prayer?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 7; Habits & Challenges specification, sections 3–5 and 10–13.

## Day 4 — Read to understand

**Aim:** Connect reading with reflection.

Give real attention to what you read. The chapter on discernment connects listening with everyday life. During your Bible reading, take time to notice an idea you would like to understand more deeply. You do not need to produce a long answer. An honest question can become the starting point for reflection.

**For reflection:** What idea or question stays with you after your reading?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 7; Habits & Challenges specification, sections 3–5 and 10–13.

## Day 5 — Honor a small responsibility

**Aim:** Recognize the value of an ordinary task.

The book illustrates faithfulness through ordinary actions: accepting a responsibility, keeping your word and continuing your work. Choose a task already in your day and give it the attention it deserves. There is no need to add obligations simply to fill a checklist. Instead, notice how you care for what has already been entrusted to you.

**For reflection:** Which everyday responsibility deserves your attention today?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 6; Habits & Challenges specification, sections 3–5 and 10–13.

## Day 6 — Care for your daily rhythm

**Aim:** Adapt the suggestions to your abilities.

Faithfulness begins with what has been entrusted to you and what you can realistically do. The module also suggests gentle movement and a rhythm of rest. Adapt these suggestions to your situation, as the program guidelines allow. Describing partial completion or asking for help is more useful than claiming an effort you did not make.

**For reflection:** Which time or level of effort needs adapting to your situation?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 6; Habits & Challenges specification, sections 3–5 and 10–13.

## Day 7 — Look back on your first week

**Aim:** Observe without condemning yourself.

Take a moment to look back on your first days. Notice an action you were able to repeat and a difficulty that keeps returning. The chapter on small things invites you to look at daily practice rather than appearances. Your review helps you prepare for what comes next. Missing an appointment erases neither what you have already done nor the possibility of beginning again.

**For reflection:** What would you like to keep, and what would you like to adjust?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 6; Habits & Challenges specification, sections 3–5 and 10–13.

## Day 8 — Connect prayer with action

**Aim:** Prepare for a practical responsibility.

The chapter on work brings prayer and action together. Bring your day into prayer, then identify a practical responsibility to take on. Depending on your circumstances, this might involve your job, your studies, a search or a family task. Difficulty finding work says nothing about your worth. Begin with what is actually possible.

**For reflection:** What practical step accompanies your prayer today?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 3; Habits & Challenges specification, sections 3–5 and 10–13.

## Day 9 — Learn to serve better

**Aim:** Give purpose to professional reading.

Professional reading is a time to learn something useful for your activity. Choose material related to your work, your studies or a real project. As you read, look for an idea you could understand or apply. The suggested duration helps you organize your time; what you record should reflect the reading you actually did.

**For reflection:** What useful idea have you taken from your reading?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 3; Habits & Challenges specification, sections 3–5 and 10–13.

## Day 10 — Keep your word

**Aim:** Review a commitment honestly.

Keeping your word is one of the examples of faithfulness in the book. Think of a specific commitment you have already made. You can honor it, clarify what remains possible or honestly let someone know if you cannot keep it. This reflection concerns a particular action. It does not ask you to define yourself by a failure.

**For reflection:** Which commitment calls for an honest response from you?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 6; Habits & Challenges specification, sections 3–5 and 10–13.

## Day 11 — Care for what is entrusted to you

**Aim:** Respect shared property or a shared responsibility.

The example of Joseph highlights care for what belongs to someone else. Consider a responsibility or an item entrusted to you: equipment, a document, a space or time. Think about how to respect it today. Quiet attention can be as meaningful as a highly visible action.

**For reflection:** What are you responsible for, even though it does not belong to you?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 6; Habits & Challenges specification, sections 3–5 and 10–13.

## Day 12 — Give one task your attention

**Aim:** Focus on an action you actually carry out.

The work described in the book calls for practical commitment. Choose a reasonable step in a task you already planned and give it your attention. Afterwards, recognize what is finished and what is still in progress. Tracking does not require you to turn a partial action into a complete success. It helps you return to the right place.

**For reflection:** Which step were you actually able to carry out today?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 3; Habits & Challenges specification, sections 3–5 and 10–13.

## Day 13 — Learn without comparing yourself

**Aim:** Focus on your own possibilities.

The reading of the parable of the talents suggested in the book highlights different abilities. Your rhythm does not have to copy someone else's. For your reading and other appointments, notice the resources you have and what you can develop. Comparison cannot replace a clear look at your own practice.

**For reflection:** What progress can you recognize without comparing yourself?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 6; Habits & Challenges specification, sections 3–5 and 10–13.

## Day 14 — Review how you organize your time

**Aim:** Choose an adjustment for the days ahead.

Look back at the appointments of this second week. Some difficulties come from unrealistic times or insufficient preparation. Choose a simple adjustment: prepare a book, move a time slot or plan better for a constraint. You can keep your completed actions while changing how you organize your time. A review does not ask you to start your whole story again.

**For reflection:** What simple adjustment would you like to try?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 6; Habits & Challenges specification, sections 3–5 and 10–13.

## Day 15 — Recognize what is already there

**Aim:** Nurture everyday gratitude.

The chapter on money distinguishes possession from attachment. During your morning or evening gratitude, take time to recognize things that cannot be measured in possessions: a relationship, something learned or help received. Recognizing these things does not require you to deny your difficulties. It simply broadens the way you look at your day.

**For reflection:** What can you be thankful for while remaining honest about your difficulties?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 4; Habits & Challenges specification, sections 3–5 and 10–13.

## Day 16 — Notice a spending habit

**Aim:** Understand a personal practice more clearly.

The book suggests observing your spending to understand your habits better. If you wish to explore this subject, privately note a specific example and what it teaches you. You do not need to enter any amount or financial detail in this module. This reflection does not replace advice suited to your situation and promises no financial outcome.

**For reflection:** What have you learned by observing this habit?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 6; Habits & Challenges specification, sections 3–5 and 10–13.

## Day 17 — Reflect on your generosity

**Aim:** Distinguish reflection from pressure to give.

The chapter on tithes and offerings opens a reflection on the place of giving. Take time to consider your motivation and your possibilities without rushing. This reading asks for no payment and does not make your progress depend on a donation. You can keep this reflection private and explore the questions that remain open.

**For reflection:** What motivates your generosity today?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 2; Habits & Challenges specification, sections 3–5 and 10–13.

## Day 18 — Choose with greater freedom

**Aim:** Examine a specific attachment.

Money can serve needs and projects; the book also invites you to examine the place it takes in your heart. Notice a desire or pressure to compare yourself that influences your choices. You can name it without judging yourself or making an immediate decision. Understanding a habit is a first step toward choosing with greater perspective.

**For reflection:** Which pressure would you like to consider with more perspective?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 4; Habits & Challenges specification, sections 3–5 and 10–13.

## Day 19 — Notice a need close to you

**Aim:** Pay attention to another person.

The chapter on helping vulnerable people brings attention back to practical needs. Notice someone around you who may need a listening ear, companionship or appropriate help. Do not disclose their situation in your activity record. Compassion begins with respectful attention; it does not require putting yourself on display.

**For reflection:** What need can you recognize respectfully and discreetly?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 5; Habits & Challenges specification, sections 3–5 and 10–13.

## Day 20 — Serve discreetly

**Aim:** Consider appropriate help, freely offered.

After recognizing a need, think about help that truly fits your possibilities and what the person wants. It might involve time, listening or a practical service. No amount of money or public proof is requested. This invitation to reflect does not automatically become a compulsory program activity.

**For reflection:** What help would be useful, welcome and within your reach?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 5; Habits & Challenges specification, sections 3–5 and 10–13.

## Day 21 — Review gratitude and care for others

**Aim:** Recognize changes in how you see things.

Look back on the past few days: how you recognize what you receive, examine your habits and pay attention to others. Mentally note what helped you and what remains difficult. The review does not turn generosity into a ranking. It helps you discern an attitude you would like to keep cultivating.

**For reflection:** Which attitude would you like to carry into the coming week?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 4; Habits & Challenges specification, sections 3–5 and 10–13.

## Day 22 — Slow down before deciding

**Aim:** Make room for discernment.

The final chapter describes the pressure of decisions made in a hurry. For an everyday decision that concerns you, take time to step back and pray. Distinguish the facts you know from the pressures you feel. This reading does not claim to convey an answer from God or to decide for you.

**For reflection:** What do you need to understand before deciding?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 7; Habits & Challenges specification, sections 3–5 and 10–13.

## Day 23 — Listen attentively

**Aim:** Connect listening to a sermon with reflection.

During your listening time, seek to understand the message and how it relates to everyday life. A sermon you choose yourself, or one recommended after review, can support your reflection. You can keep one idea and one question in mind. Opening a link or starting an audio recording does not, by itself, mean you have listened.

**For reflection:** Which idea deserves further thought after listening?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 7; Habits & Challenges specification, sections 3–5 and 10–13.

## Day 24 — Do not confuse speed with faithfulness

**Aim:** Choose a sustainable rhythm.

A quick decision is not always a better understood decision. Look at your schedule and the appointments you want to keep. You can distinguish a real constraint from a sense of urgency you have added. Maintaining a sustainable rhythm helps you stay attentive to what you do, without turning every delay into a judgment about yourself.

**For reflection:** Which sense of urgency deserves another look?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 7; Habits & Challenges specification, sections 3–5 and 10–13.

## Day 25 — Prepare to attend worship

**Aim:** Adapt the time slots to your congregation.

The module plans for three worship gatherings each week, with days and times you can customize. Check that your time slots match your congregation's actual schedule and your own constraints. You report your attendance yourself; it is not inferred from your presence in the app. You should be able to report honestly when attendance is not possible.

**For reflection:** Does your worship schedule fit what you can realistically do?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 6; Habits & Challenges specification, sections 3–5 and 10–13.

## Day 26 — Begin again after a missed appointment

**Aim:** Continue without erasing the journey so far.

A missed appointment calls for a practical response rather than erasing your efforts. Look at what made it difficult and prepare for the next available opportunity. You can record partial completion or ask for help. In this tracking system, beginning again does not mean deleting your reading, prayers or other actions already confirmed.

**For reflection:** Which next appointment can you prepare for simply?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 6; Habits & Challenges specification, sections 3–5 and 10–13.

## Day 27 — Recognize when you need help

**Aim:** Name a difficulty without defining yourself by it.

Regular practice can reveal obstacles you had not anticipated. Identify a specific difficulty: timing, understanding, tiredness or organization. You can ask for help without claiming to have finished the activity. Asking for help remains separate from completion and does not reset your other actions.

**For reflection:** Where would help be useful to you?

Take an overall look at these four weeks. Notice the appointments that have become easier to prepare for and those that still need adapting. Base your review on what you actually recorded, without comparing your numbers with anyone else's. The review describes a practice; it does not pass judgment on your spiritual life.

**For reflection:** What would you like to continue, and what do you need to adapt?

**Aim:** Choose what you would like to continue.

Return to your priorities and the habits that put them into practice. Choose what you would like to continue after this first period and what you need to make that possible. **Aim:** Recognize the journey so far and a next step.

Look back on your journey through these thirty days. Recognize an action you repeated, something you learned and a difficulty that remains. You can give thanks for what has grown and choose a realistic next step. The end of this period does not erase your history or trigger an automatic payment. Your review remains a foundation for what comes next.

**For reflection:** What next step do you choose with confidence and realism?

# Weekly challenges (days 1 to 30)

The challenges come back with the readings in months 2 to 6. They are read only: no data is collected.

## Challenge, days 1 to 7 — Prepare a faithful appointment

**Aim:** Choose a realistic daily appointment and observe how you practice it.

This week, choose among your habits one appointment you want to prepare better: prayer, reading or gratitude. Check its time and plan what can help you enter into it calmly. Each time, declare only what you actually did. If you miss it, prepare the next one; you are not starting from zero.

**Adapting:** You can move the time, prepare your material in advance or ask for help to understand the activity. Partial completion is still useful information.

**Looking back:** What made this appointment easier, and which adjustment do you want to keep?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapters 1 and 6; Habits & Challenges specification, sections 4 and 9.

## Challenge, days 8 to 14 — Take care of a responsibility

**Aim:** Bring attention and honesty to an existing responsibility.

Choose a responsibility already present in your life: work, study, a family task or a personal commitment. Define one concrete step you can reasonably carry out this week. Take time to prepare it and to recognize what is finished or still in progress. This is not about piling up tasks to get a better score.

**Adapting:** If your schedule changes, make the step smaller or explain what was not possible. This challenge does not make your worth depend on a job, an income or a performance.

**Looking back:** What new attention have you given to this responsibility?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapters 3 and 6; Habits & Challenges specification, section 9.

## Challenge, days 15 to 21 — Practice attentive generosity

**Aim:** Reflect on help that is free, respectful and suited to your means.

Notice a need around you without exposing the person concerned. If they wish it and if you can, offer suitable help: listening, doing a favor, giving time or choosing another gesture. Also take a moment to reflect on what motivates your generosity. This challenge asks for no payment.

**Adapting:** You can choose a non-financial gesture or keep this week as a time of listening and reflection. Do not write a name, an amount or a private situation in a challenge declaration.

**Looking back:** What have you learned about a respectful way of helping?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapters 2, 4 and 5; Habits & Challenges specification, sections 9 and 12.

## Challenge, days 22 to 28 — Make room for discernment

**Aim:** Step back before an ordinary decision.

Choose an ordinary decision you can step back from a little. Give it a time of prayer, separate the known facts from the pressure you feel, and put into words the question that remains open. The program does not claim to receive a divine answer in your place. It invites you to practice a more peaceful attention.

**Adapting:** You can keep a question open or ask for appropriate advice. This challenge does not replace professional help for a decision that needs it.

**Looking back:** What have you understood better by slowing down?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 7; Habits & Challenges specification, sections 9 and 13.

## Review, days 29 and 30 — Choose what comes next with clarity

Read over one thing you learned, one difficulty and one habit you want to continue. Choose a realistic next step. This review triggers neither renewal nor payment.

**Questions:** What would you like to keep? What help do you still need? Which next step do you choose?
