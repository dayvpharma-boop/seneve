Month 2 went through the whole book, one key after another. Month 3 returns in depth to the first two keys: God first (days 61 to 73), then tithes and offerings (days 74 to 89), with a review on day 90. Days 65, 72, 79 and 86 review the week.

Sentences in quotation marks are quotations from the book or from the Bible; the rest is editorial adaptation to be validated. No text promises a miracle, healing or wealth, or measures a person's faith or worth. The book's stories about the tithe are presented without any promise of results, and no amount is asked of you. The questions invite reflection; they do not ask you to type anything. Daily habits and the three prayers are still tracked separately.

## Day 61 — Change the causes

**Aim:** Ask God to change the causes, not only the consequences.

At the beginning of the book, the author tells of a night of debt and fear. He prayed then: "Lord, do something. Lord, send a miracle." Later, alone in his living room, he prayed differently: "Lord, I am no longer asking You to erase the consequences. Teach me to change the causes." He then repaid his debts over three years, with no shortcut. Think of a difficulty you are carrying. Instead of only asking for it to disappear, ask God to show you what, on your side, can change.

**For reflection:** Which cause, on your side, would you like God to help you change?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 1.

## Day 62 — Live in the Kingdom, do not visit it

**Aim:** Let Sunday's faith reach Monday.

Paul writes that God "hath translated us into the kingdom of his dear Son" (Colossians 1:13). The book draws a picture from it: you do not visit the Kingdom on Sunday, you reside in it. It describes the citizen of the Kingdom like this: "His Monday looks like his Sunday." Today, choose an ordinary place in your day — the market, the office, the road or your home — and ask yourself how your faith can simply be seen there.

**For reflection:** In which place of your week is your faith hardest to see?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 1.

## Day 63 — Religion or Kingdom

**Aim:** See where you stand honestly, one line at a time.

Chapter 1 offers a table in two columns. On one side: praying but deciding alone, seeking the blessing, living in constant urgency. On the other: praying and waiting for God's direction, seeking His will, keeping peace while waiting. The book invites you to tick honestly, line by line, the column that resembles you: "It is between you and God." Do it today for just one line, without condemning yourself. If you recognize yourself in the first column, it is not an ending: it is the place to begin.

**For reflection:** Which line of the table would you like to see change in your life?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 1.

## Day 64 — Consult before you sign

**Aim:** Take time to pray before a hurried decision.

Through the prophet Isaiah, God rebuked His people because they "take counsel, but not of me" (Isaiah 30:1). The book tells the story of Marie-Claire, a nurse in Lagos, who was offered double the salary. Everyone pressed her to sign quickly. She took three days to pray, did not find peace, and declined. She later said: "God takes care of those who consult Him." Before your next important decision, set aside a real time of prayer.

**For reflection:** Which decision deserves a few days of prayer before you answer?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 1.

## Day 65 — Review the week: the King rather than the assistant

**Aim:** Take stock of a week of consulting God.

This week spoke about changing the causes rather than only running from the consequences, living your faith on Monday as on Sunday, looking honestly at the table that compares religion and the Kingdom, and consulting God before deciding. Read over your records without looking for a score. Notice one decision where you took time to pray, even briefly. This is often how habits change: through small, repeated moments.

**For reflection:** Which decision this week were you able to present to God?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 1; Habits & Challenges specification, section 9.

## Day 66 — One master, less worry

**Aim:** Connect God's first place with inner peace.

The book reads Matthew 6 in order. Jesus says first: "Ye cannot serve God and mammon" (v. 24). Then: "Take no thought for your life" (v. 25). Then: "But seek ye first the kingdom of God" (v. 33). One master, so less worry, so one single priority. The book adds: "Your Father knoweth what you have need of." Today, when a worry comes back, name it before God instead of keeping it to yourself.

**For reflection:** Which worry do you want to entrust to God today?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 1.

## Day 67 — Seek before you run

**Aim:** Take time to seek God in a difficult season.

The book tells the story of Patrice, from Nairobi, who lost his job while he had a wife and three children. Instead of accepting anything in a panic, he took forty days to seek God seriously, in prayer. The book makes clear: "I do not promise you that your story will be identical." What matters here is the order: seek before you run. If you are going through a time of uncertainty, set aside a specific time each day to seek God, without giving up your practical steps.

**For reflection:** In which situation are you tempted to run before you seek?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 1.

## Day 68 — The "afterward" trap

**Aim:** Notice what you have put off until later.

The book describes Mammon's strategy: it does not ask you to deny God, only to postpone. "Take care of your own affairs first. Get yourself stable first. You will serve God afterward." It tells the story of a faithful printer whose deliveries gradually took over his Sundays: "it's temporary," he said. Today, without accusing yourself, look at what you have put off until "afterward" in your life with God. Choose one single thing to take up again this week.

**For reflection:** What have you put off until later in your relationship with God?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 1.

## Day 69 — The home, the first territory

**Aim:** Let faith be lived at home.

The book says that the Kingdom is expressed first "in your house." It quotes Deuteronomy: to speak of the Word "when thou sittest in thine house, and when thou walkest by the way, and when thou liest down, and when thou risest up" (Deuteronomy 6:7). Faith is passed on in everyday life, not only in gatherings. Today, share a simple moment at home: a prayer, a verse, a thank you. If you live alone, this moment can be shared with someone close to you, by a call.

**For reflection:** What small act of faith could you share at home this week?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 1.

## Day 70 — Forgiveness rather than long silences

**Aim:** Take a first step of peace with someone close to you.

The book describes a healthy Christian home: it resolves its conflicts "through grace and forgiveness — not through shouting or silences that last for weeks." It speaks of children who see their parents ask each other's forgiveness when they have hurt each other. Think of a tension with someone close to you, at home or elsewhere. Today, take a modest first step: a word, an apology, a question asked calmly. If the situation puts you in danger, seek help instead from someone you trust.

**For reflection:** With whom would you like to take a first step of peace?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 1.

## Day 71 — Today's obedience

**Aim:** Do one thing God has already put on your heart.

Samuel says to Saul: "To obey is better than sacrifice" (1 Samuel 15:22). The book observes that those who move forward are not necessarily the most educated or the best connected: "They are the most obedient." It adds a freeing word: "You do not need to be perfect to begin." And it ends the chapter with a question: "what has God already asked of you that you have not yet done?" Choose one small step, and take it today.

**For reflection:** Which step, already on your heart, can you take today?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 1.

## Day 72 — Review the week: order restored

**Aim:** Take stock of order, the home and obedience.

This week spoke about entrusting your worries, seeking before running, the "afterward" trap, the home, forgiveness and obedience. Read over your records without judging yourself. Notice a moment when you put something back in its place, even something small. The journey moves forward through simple, repeated decisions, and starting again after forgetting is part of it.

**For reflection:** What did you put back in its place this week?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 1; Habits & Challenges specification, section 9.

## Day 73 — I change the order

**Aim:** Name before God one area to hand over to Him.

Chapter 1 ends with a prayer: "Today, I change the order. I place You first — in my time, in my money, in my decisions, in my relationships." The written commitment that follows invites you to "surrender to God the specific area where I have resisted Him until now." Take a moment to pray in your own words. If you wish, write this area on a piece of paper that stays yours: SÉNEVÉ does not ask you for it.

**For reflection:** Which area do you want to hand over to God first?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 1.

## Day 74 — The last area surrendered

**Aim:** Look calmly at your relationship with money.

Chapter 2 begins with an observation: "The wallet is the last domain a man surrenders to God." You can give up many things and keep your money closed to God. The author also acknowledges that abuses committed in the name of the tithe have created mistrust. He makes clear that he is not speaking about what the church wants, but about what God has established. Today, simply notice whether your money is part of what you entrust to God in prayer.

**For reflection:** What do you feel when money is talked about at church, and why?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 2.

## Day 75 — Recognize where the victory comes from

**Aim:** Connect what you receive with the One who gives it.

The book recalls that Abraham gave the tithe to Melchizedek after his victory (Genesis 14), four centuries before the Law of Moses. No one had commanded it of him. The book sees in it "the natural response of a man who understands that his victory comes from God." Think of a recent success, small or great: an exam, a finished piece of work, a problem solved, some help received. Thank God specifically for it today.

**For reflection:** Which recent success do you want to recognize as a gift from God?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 2.

## Day 76 — A vow born of grace

**Aim:** Give out of gratitude rather than constraint.

At Bethel, after his vision of the ladder, Jacob makes this vow: "of all that thou shalt give me I will surely give the tenth unto thee" (Genesis 28:22). The book points out that no written commandment obliged him: "Only a heart touched by grace." Here the gift comes after an encounter with God. Today, before thinking about what you give, take time to remember what God has done for you.

**For reflection:** Which memory of God's faithfulness makes you grateful?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 2.

## Day 77 — Without neglecting justice

**Aim:** Keep giving and a just heart together.

The book quotes Matthew 23:23, where Jesus speaks of the tithe and adds: "these ought ye to have done, and not to leave the other undone." In this passage, those other things are justice, mercy and faith. For the book, the tithe is not a religious tax but an act of justice. Giving never replaces the way we treat others. Today, do an act of justice or mercy toward someone.

**For reflection:** Where are justice and mercy asked of you this week?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 2.

## Day 78 — Pray for those who feed you

**Aim:** Recognize those who watch over your spiritual life.

The book recalls that the tithe once supported the Levites, consecrated to the service of God (Numbers 18:21), and quotes Paul: "Even so hath the Lord ordained that they which preach the gospel should live of the gospel" (1 Corinthians 9:14). It asks a practical question: "Who feeds your soul?" It also advises wisdom: connect to a ministry that is faithful to the Word. Today, pray for someone who watches over you in prayer, and thank them if you can.

**For reflection:** Who is feeding your soul at the moment?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 2.

## Day 79 — Review the week: giving with gratitude

**Aim:** Take stock of gratitude and giving.

This week spoke about the way to giving: handing over your order and your money to God, recognizing where the victory comes from, giving out of gratitude, keeping justice and praying for those who feed you. Read over your records without comparing yourself to anyone. No amount is expected of you here. Above all, notice whether the way you look at what you receive has changed.

**For reflection:** What nourished your gratitude this week?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapters 1 and 2; Habits & Challenges specification, section 9.

## Day 80 — The first portion in lack

**Aim:** Reflect on trust when resources are low.

The book revisits the story of the widow of Zarephath (1 Kings 17). She had only a handful of flour and a little oil, and Elijah asked her to prepare for him first. She obeyed, and the Word says: "the barrel of meal wasted not." For the book, scarcity is often the place where faith is shown. This story sets no amount for you. It invites you to look at where you trust God when you have little.

**For reflection:** In which lack do you find it hardest to trust God?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 2.

## Day 81 — No longer afraid of lack

**Aim:** Receive peace before anything else.

The book tells the story of Cécile, in Kinshasa, who set aside her tithe on a gift of one hundred dollars during a very hard time. She later said: "I am not saying the tithe caused the contract." What she holds on to is something else: "I have stopped being afraid of lack." The book calls this the first blessing: being set free from fear. Today, name before God a fear related to money and ask Him for His peace.

**For reflection:** Which fear related to money do you want to entrust to God?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 2.

## Day 82 — What God looks at

**Aim:** Understand that God sees the heart more than the sum.

At the temple, Jesus watches rich people put in large sums, then a widow who gives two small coins. He says: "This poor widow hath cast more in than all they." The book explains: the rich had given out of their surplus; she had given what she had to live on. "He sees what you kept, not only what you gave." These words are not a reproach. They invite you to look honestly at what you hold back: money, time or attention.

**For reflection:** What do you find hard to let go of at the moment?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 2.

## Day 83 — Faith, not the lottery

**Aim:** Give without trying to oblige God.

The book warns against giving the way one plays the lottery: "I sowed a thousand dollars, God owes me ten thousand." Its answer is clear: "You do not buy God." You do not oblige Him; you align your life with His principles and trust in His faithfulness. If someone promises you a gain in exchange for a gift, remember this page. Giving remains an act of trust and love, never a bargain.

**For reflection:** What moves you to give, when you give?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 2.

## Day 84 — What disappears before the fifteenth

**Aim:** Notice the leaks and entrust what remains.

Malachi 3:11 speaks of "the devourer." The book applies it to a familiar experience: "you receive your salary, and before the fifteenth of the month, everything has vanished." It tells the story of Thomas, a restaurant owner, who decided to give to God first and said: "there seems to be a protection over what remains." This story does not promise that nothing unexpected will happen. Today, entrust what you have to God, and spot one unexpected expense that often comes back, so you can prepare for it better.

**For reflection:** Which unexpected expense often comes back in your budget?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 2.

## Day 85 — A proportion, not an amount

**Aim:** Understand that faithfulness does not depend on the sum.

To the objection "I don't have enough to give the tithe," the book answers: "The tithe is not an amount — it is a proportion. Ten percent of one thousand is one hundred. Ten percent of five hundred is fifty." It concludes: "Faithfulness does not depend on the amount." If you practice tithing, the module's optional simulator can help you with this calculation; it saves nothing and starts no payment.

**For reflection:** What makes faithfulness possible, even with little?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 2.

**Aim:** Take stock of trust and the fear of lack.

This week spoke about trust in lack, the fear of lack, what God looks at, refusing to bargain with Him, money leaks and faithfulness that does not depend on the amount. Read over what you lived without judging yourself. The book reminds us that we cannot force God, but we can trust Him. Note one fear that has calmed a little, or another that still needs time.

## Day 87 — When others' example disappoints

**Aim:** Do not let the abuse of some decide for you.

The book hears the objection: "Pastors get rich off our tithes." It answers: "Some do, yes." Then it adds that the abuse of a few does not cancel the principle, just as we do not stop marrying because some marriages fail. It advises looking for a ministry that is faithful to the Word. If a disappointment weighs on you, speak to God about it honestly and, if it helps, to someone you trust.

**For reflection:** Which disappointment may have held back your trust?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 2.

## Day 88 — A covenant, not a transaction

**Aim:** Look at faithfulness over time.

To the objection "I don't see immediate results," the book answers: "The tithe is a covenant, not a transaction." A covenant is verified over years, not over weeks. It adds with humor: "God's accountants do not close their books at the end of the month." This patience also applies to your habits: a missed day does not undo a journey. Simply start again where you are.

**For reflection:** Where do you need patience at the moment?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 2.

## Day 89 — Everything is a loan

**Aim:** Receive what you have as entrusted by God.

The prayer in chapter 2 says: "My salary, my profits, my health, my intelligence — all of it is a loan from Your hand." Seeing what you have as a loan changes the way you manage it: with care, with gratitude and without fear. Tonight, during your gratitude, name three things that have been entrusted to you and ask God for wisdom to take care of them.

**For reflection:** Which three things have been entrusted to you?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 2.

## Day 90 — Where your treasure is

**Aim:** Make a peaceful review of the month and choose one step.

Chapter 2 ends with this sentence: "Where your treasure is, your heart will always end up dwelling." This month spoke about God's first place, order, the home, obedience and giving. Read over your weekly reviews and choose a single step for next month. Your review measures neither your faith nor your worth. Your daily habits continue, and you can adjust your times at any moment.

**For reflection:** Which step do you want to keep for next month?
