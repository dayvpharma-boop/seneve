The month follows the order of the book, one key after another: God first (days 31 to 34), tithes and offerings (35 to 38), work (39 to 42), money (43 to 48), Tzedakah (49 to 52), faithfulness in the little things (53 to 55), the leading of the Spirit (56, 57, 59), then choosing one key for what comes next (60). Days 37, 44, 51 and 58 review the week.

Sentences in quotation marks are quotations from the book or from the Bible; the rest is editorial adaptation to be validated. No text promises a miracle, healing or wealth, or measures a person's faith or worth. The questions invite reflection; they do not ask you to type anything. Daily habits and the three prayers are still tracked separately.

## Day 31 — Look at who comes first

**Aim:** Honestly observe what holds first place.

The book opens with a single question: "Who is truly first in your life?" It is not about what you sing on Sunday, but about what your decisions, your schedule and your money show. Today, simply observe your day, without judging yourself. Where do your first minutes, your first thoughts, your first expenses go? This honest look is not a grade: it is a starting point to put things back in order, step by step.

**For reflection:** What received your first minutes this morning?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 1.

## Day 32 — King or assistant

**Aim:** Move from "Lord, bless what I am doing" to "Lord, what do You want me to do?"

The book contrasts two prayers. One says: "Lord, bless what I am doing." The other asks: "Lord, what do You want me to do?" The first has already decided everything; the second waits for direction. The story of Simon, in chapter 1, shows what happens when intelligence becomes king and God a mere assistant. Choose one decision in your day and present it to God before you make it, in your own words.

**For reflection:** Which decision do you want to present to God before you make it?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 1.

## Day 33 — Entrust your schedule

**Aim:** Connect your prayer time with the rest of your day.

The book warns against a comfortable idea: that having prayed in the morning is enough to make God first. It offers another picture: "the true Christian does not put God into his schedule — he puts his schedule into God's hands." Your prayer time remains precious; it can also become the moment when you look at your day with Him. "In all thy ways acknowledge him" (Proverbs 3:6). Before you begin your activities, go over your plans in prayer.

**For reflection:** Which moment of your day do you find hardest to entrust to God?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 1.

## Day 34 — Put your priorities in order

**Aim:** See where you stand in the order of priorities the book proposes.

Chapter 1 proposes an order: God, your personal spiritual life, your home, your work or studies, your service in the church, then everything else. It reminds us that the home comes before church activities (1 Timothy 3:4-5). Look at your week in the light of this order. The point is not to overturn everything at once, but to notice one area that has taken too much room, or not enough.

**For reflection:** Which area of your life deserves more attention this week?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 1.

## Day 35 — The first portion rather than the leftovers

**Aim:** Understand the idea of "firstfruits".

The book tells the story of Brother Évariste, who calculated his tithe on what was left after all his expenses — often nothing. Chapter 2 recalls Proverbs 3:9: to honour the Lord "with the firstfruits of all thine increase", that is, "the first portion, not the leftovers." If you practise tithing, notice at what moment you set it aside. The module's optional simulator can help you with the calculation; it saves nothing and starts no payment.

**For reflection:** At what moment do you set aside what you give to God?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 2; complementary teaching on the tithe validated by Dr Justin (Habits & Challenges specification, section 8).

## Day 36 — Tell the tithe and the offering apart

**Aim:** Distinguish what is returned from what is offered.

The book distinguishes two acts. The tithe is a restitution: ten percent, returned because it already belongs to God. The offering is a free gift, beyond the tithe, with no fixed amount. Jesus noticed the widow who gave two small coins (Mark 12): "God weighs the sacrifice, not the amount," the book says. No amount is expected of you here. Simply reflect on the spirit in which you give.

**For reflection:** What makes a gift truly free for you?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 2.

## Day 37 — Review the week: first place

**Aim:** Take stock of a week of priorities.

This week spoke about first place: the question "Who is truly first in your life?", the prayer that asks for direction, the order of priorities and the first portion of what you receive. Read over your records without looking for a score. Note what has changed, even slightly, and what remains difficult. As the book reminds us: "God is not looking for the perfect man. He is looking for the obedient man." A modest adjustment that you keep is worth more than a big resolution you abandon.

**For reflection:** Which small change of priority do you want to keep next week?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 1; Habits & Challenges specification, section 9.

## Day 38 — Give without seeking attention

**Aim:** Examine your motives when you give.

Chapter 2 describes three traps that empty a gift of its meaning: giving to be seen, giving while grumbling, giving "to force God's hand". Jesus invites us to give without letting the left hand know what the right hand is doing (Matthew 6:3). Paul reminds us that "God loveth a cheerful giver" (2 Corinthians 9:7). The book says it plainly: "You do not buy God." Notice which of these traps threatens you most, without accusing yourself.

**For reflection:** Which of these three traps do you recognise most easily in yourself?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 2.

## Day 39 — Consecrated effort

**Aim:** Hold work and prayer together.

Chapter 3 describes two imbalances: working without praying, and praying without working. The book sums it up in two words: "consecrated effort." Effort, because you act; consecrated, because you pray. Today, before your work or your professional reading, entrust your task to God in one sentence. Then do it seriously, with what you have.

**For reflection:** Where do you lean more: working without praying, or praying without acting?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 3.

## Day 40 — Work is not a punishment

**Aim:** See your work as a calling.

The book recalls that work existed before the fall: God put man in the garden "to dress it and to keep it" (Genesis 2:15). It also recalls that in Nazareth, people said of Jesus: "Is not this the carpenter?" (Mark 6:3). No honest work is unworthy. Whether your work is visible or hidden, paid or not, look at it today as a way of serving.

**For reflection:** Which part of your work do you tend to look down on?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 3.

## Day 41 — When no one is watching

**Aim:** Check your integrity in the small acts of work.

Chapter 3 speaks of what happens at work when no one is watching: the hours reported, the borrowed equipment, the adjusted report. It quotes Daniel, of whom Scripture says: "neither was there any error or fault found in him" (Daniel 6:4). Today, choose one concrete, small act of honesty: correct a mistake, return what is not yours, present a result as it really is. If something needs to be made right, simply do it.

**For reflection:** What small act of honesty can you do at work today?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 3.

## Day 42 — Your work speaks before you do

**Aim:** Consider the effect of your behaviour on others.

The book says that "your life has already preached before your mouth." Arriving on time, keeping your word, giving the exact change, respecting the least: these acts are noticed. They can open conversations without speeches. Today, do not try to preach. Be reliable in one thing others expect from you.

**For reflection:** What do your colleagues or your family see of your faith in the way you work?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 3.

## Day 43 — Money, a tool and not a master

**Aim:** Distinguish money from attachment to money.

Chapter 4 corrects a verse that is often misquoted: it is not money, but "the love of money" that "is the root of all evil" (1 Timothy 6:10). Money is a tool; attachment can become an idol. The book notes that you can own a lot without being owned, and have little while being obsessed with money. Today, notice the place money takes in your thoughts, without guilt.

**For reflection:** How many times did you think about money today, and in what frame of mind?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 4.

## Day 44 — Review the week: giving, work and money

**Aim:** Take stock of giving, work and money.

This week spoke about giving freely, working with prayer and honesty, and the place of money. Read over what you recorded, and what you could not do, without judging yourself. If you noticed an area to correct, choose just one simple action for next week. If something weighs on you, you can ask for help at any time.

**For reflection:** Which simple action do you want to continue next week?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapters 2 to 4; Habits & Challenges specification, section 9.

## Day 45 — When your peace depends on your balance

**Aim:** Spot a sign of attachment to money.

The book describes several signs of a heart too attached to money. The first: a peace that rises and falls with your account. Others follow: praying less when you earn more, making compromises to protect what you have, finding it hard to give, being disturbed by the success of others. "A single one is enough to begin the cleanup," it says. Choose one sign and observe it honestly today.

**For reflection:** Which of these signs do you recognise, even a little, in your life?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 4.

## Day 46 — Gratitude against covetousness

**Aim:** Practise specific gratitude.

Chapter 4 offers a simple remedy: each evening, name before God three things received during the day. "Gratitude is the natural antidote to covetousness," the book says. Tonight, during your gratitude, look for specific things rather than general ones: a meal, a word, some help, a moment of calm.

**For reflection:** What three specific things did you receive today?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 4.

## Day 47 — Hands full, but open

**Aim:** Reflect on what you hold on to.

The book compares two rich men: Abraham — "And Abraham rose up early in the morning" (Genesis 22:3) — when God asked him for Isaac, and the rich young man, who went away sad (Mark 10). It highlights one detail: before speaking, "Jesus beholding him loved him" (Mark 10:21). God does not seek to take, but to set free. What matters is not the amount, but the ability to let go. Think about what you hold most tightly.

**For reflection:** What would you find hardest to hand over to God?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 4.

## Day 48 — Choose simplicity

**Aim:** Tell your needs apart from the image you want to give.

Chapter 4 speaks of a time when people sometimes go into debt to keep up appearances. The book makes clear that luxury is not a sin, but that the need to appear can become a prison. It offers three questions: "Do I buy for my needs, or for the eyes of others? Does my standard of living match my real income, or the image I want to project? Could I live more simply — and give more?" Take the time to answer one of them honestly.

**For reflection:** Which of these three questions concerns you most today?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 4.

## Day 49 — Be a channel rather than a reservoir

**Aim:** Understand Tzedakah as justice.

Chapter 5 presents the Hebrew word Tzedakah, which means justice rather than charity. The book uses an image: God has blessed you "so that you may be a channel, not a reservoir." The reservoir keeps the water until it stagnates; the channel lets it flow and stays alive. Helping those in need then becomes a just way of passing on what you have received. Think of someone close to you who is going through a difficulty.

**For reflection:** Does what you receive flow on to others, or does it stay with you?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 5.

## Day 50 — Give in secret

**Aim:** Do an act of kindness without making it known.

Jesus teaches us to give in secret: "thy Father which seeth in secret himself shall reward thee openly" (Matthew 6:4). The book tells of an envelope received one evening from a man who never made himself known, at the exact moment of a hidden need. This week, try a discreet act: some help, a service, a word, without photos or announcements. You do not have to tell anyone.

**For reflection:** What discreet act could you do this week?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 5.

## Day 51 — Review the week: a freer heart

**Aim:** Take stock of money, gratitude and sharing.

This week spoke about the signs of attachment to money, gratitude, open hands, simplicity and sharing. Read over your moments of gratitude and what you were able to give, in money, time or attention. There is no right amount. Simply notice whether the way you look at money and at others has changed a little.

**For reflection:** What changed in the way you look at money this week?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapters 4 and 5; Habits & Challenges specification, section 9.

## Day 52 — Time and presence are gifts

**Aim:** Give something other than money.

The book answers the objection "I have no money to give": "Time is a currency. Presence is a currency. Skills are a currency." A widow needs someone to listen to her; a young person needs someone to teach them a trade; an isolated sick person is waiting for a face. Choose one person and offer them some of your time this week.

**For reflection:** Who could you offer an hour of presence to this week?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 5.

## Day 53 — Faithful in what belongs to another

**Aim:** Take care of what does not belong to you.

Chapter 6 follows Joseph in Potiphar's house: he manages another man's goods with care. Jesus says: "And if ye have not been faithful in that which is another man's, who shall give you that which is your own?" (Luke 16:12). What belongs to another is also your employer's time, the borrowed tool, the word you gave. Today, return an item, respect a schedule or keep a promise made to someone.

**For reflection:** What has been entrusted to you that deserves more care?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 6.

## Day 54 — Notice sad faces

**Aim:** Pay attention to someone around you.

In prison, Joseph notices that two fellow prisoners look downcast and asks them: "Wherefore look ye so sadly to day?" (Genesis 40:7). The book points out that this small attention changed his story. Faithfulness also concerns people: a tired colleague, a lonely neighbour, a worried relative. Today, take the time to sincerely ask someone how they are.

**For reflection:** Who around you seems to be carrying something heavy right now?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 6.

## Day 55 — Know where your money goes

**Aim:** Observe your spending without judging it.

The book suggests an exercise: "for thirty days, change nothing in your habits — but write everything down." Many discover "invisible leaks", such as small impulse purchases. Chapter 6 also recalls the example of Joseph, who had part of the harvest set aside during the years of plenty (Genesis 41:34). If you wish, start a personal notebook today. It stays yours: SÉNEVÉ does not ask for your amounts.

**For reflection:** Which expense would you like to understand better this month?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 6.

## Day 56 — Begin with silence

**Aim:** Make a time of calm before the screens.

Chapter 7 recalls that God was not in the wind, nor in the earthquake, nor in the fire, but in "a still small voice" (1 Kings 19). The book advises you to "begin each day with ten to twenty minutes of silence before God — before the phone." Tomorrow morning, try not to look at your phone before your prayer.

**For reflection:** What fills the first quarter of an hour of your days?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 7.

## Day 57 — Slow down when urgency rises

**Aim:** Recognise the pressure that pushes you to decide too quickly.

The book distinguishes two voices: "Urgency pushes; the Spirit leads. Urgency creates anxiety; the Spirit gives peace." The author tells how contracts signed under pressure contributed to his own debts. He drew a rule from it: when the pressure to decide immediately becomes strong, "that is precisely the signal to slow down." Then pray and seek counsel. Think of a decision that is pressing on you right now.

**For reflection:** Which decision deserves more of your time?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 7.

## Day 58 — Look back over four weeks

**Aim:** See the road travelled this month.

This month has gone through the whole book: God first, tithes and offerings, work, money, sharing, faithfulness and the leading of the Spirit. Read over your weekly reviews. Notice what you have understood, what you have begun, and what remains difficult. The journey is not a race: starting again after a break is also part of faithfulness.

**For reflection:** Which part of this month spoke to you most?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, general conclusion; Habits & Challenges specification, section 9.

## Day 59 — Five questions before deciding

**Aim:** Give yourself markers for an important decision.

The book offers five questions to ask before any important decision. Do I have deep and lasting peace about this? Is this direction consistent with the Word of God? Are there independent providential confirmations? What do spiritually mature and competent people say? And: "if I remove the financial gain from this decision, would I still make the same choice?" Keep these questions close for your next important decision.

**For reflection:** Which question would help you most with a decision you are preparing?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 7.

## Day 60 — Choose one key for what comes next

**Aim:** Make a peaceful review and choose a single step.

At the end of the book, the author invites you: "Choose one key — just one to begin." Among the seven markers of this month, which one do you want to practise first next month? Your review measures neither your faith nor your worth; it helps you see where you are. Your daily habits continue, and you can adjust your times at any moment.

**For reflection:** Which key do you want to choose for next month?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, general conclusion.
