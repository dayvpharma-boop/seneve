Month 4 returns in depth to two keys: work (days 91 to 105, chapter 3), then money and the heart (days 106 to 119, chapter 4), with a review on day 120. Days 93, 100, 107 and 114 review the week (one review every 7 days, as since month 2). Passages already used in month 2 are not repeated.

Sentences in quotation marks are quotations from the book or from the Bible; the rest is editorial adaptation to be validated. No text promises a miracle, healing, promotion or wealth, or measures a person's faith or worth. The book's stories are presented without any promise of results, and no amount is asked of you. The texts about debt invite you to seek counsel and do not replace professional advice. The questions invite reflection; they do not ask you to type anything.

## Day 91 — A God who works

**Aim:** See your honest work as a way of resembling God.

Chapter 3 opens with a short sentence: "God does not rain down loaves. He blesses hands." The book also recalls a word of Jesus: "My Father worketh hitherto, and I work" (John 5:17). The God of the Bible is not an idle God. When you work honestly — at home, in the fields, at the office or at school — you resemble Him. Today, begin an ordinary task by entrusting it to God.

**For reflection:** Which ordinary task in your day do you want to do with God?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 3.

## Day 92 — More than surviving

**Aim:** Find the meaning of your work beyond tiredness.

The book distinguishes work from its hardship. The fall did not create work; it added to it "the sweat, the resistance, the thorn." In Christ, it says, work recovers its dignity: "You no longer work only to survive — you work to glorify God." Your tiredness is real, and it matters to God. Today, name before Him the hardest part of your work, and ask Him for strength to do it with meaning.

**For reflection:** Which part of your work weighs on you most at the moment?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 3.

## Day 93 — Review the week: from covenant to work

**Aim:** Take stock of the week that opens the fourth month.

This week linked two months: the patience of a covenant, everything entrusted to you as a loan, the treasure that draws the heart, then work, where God blesses hands. Read over your records without judging yourself. Notice a moment when you worked or served with God, even briefly. Each new month is a chance to simply start again.

**For reflection:** Which moment of work were you able to live with God this week?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapters 2 and 3; Habits & Challenges specification, section 9.

## Day 94 — The small door

**Aim:** Do not look down on modest work.

The book tells the story of Gaston, out of work for two years, who refused positions "beneath his level" while waiting for "the great door." In the end he accepted a job as a delivery man, and did it with punctuality and honesty. The book draws a picture from it: "the great door did indeed exist. It was simply hidden behind the small one." If you are looking for your place, look honestly at the small doors that are already opening.

**For reflection:** Which small door may you have set aside too quickly?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 3.

## Day 95 — Encourage someone who is starting again

**Aim:** Support someone who is taking courage again.

On the third day of his new job, Gaston was mocked by a former classmate and wanted to give up. It was his wife who lifted him back up: "Today, for the first time in two years, you look like a man on the move. Don't stop." A right word, at the right moment, can help someone hold on. Think of a person who is starting something difficult again, and give them a word of encouragement today.

**For reflection:** Who needs a word of encouragement from you?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 3.

## Day 96 — Without guile

**Aim:** Be upright in the small details of work.

Of Jesus, Scripture says: "neither was guile found in his mouth" (1 Peter 2:22). The book imagines the carpenter of Nazareth: "Not one crooked plank sold as straight, not one inflated word." It offers a simple table: when you are late, own it and put it right; when there is a mistake in a report, point it out. Today, choose one detail of your work to make more just: an hour, a figure, a promise.

**For reflection:** Which detail of your work would you like to make more just?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 3.

## Day 97 — Launch out into the deep

**Aim:** Move from waiting to a concrete step.

Peter fished all night and caught nothing. Jesus says to him: "Launch out into the deep, and let down your nets for a draught." The book points out that the Master does not create fish while Peter sleeps: "He says: launch out. Cast. Act." Peter had to row, cast and haul. Think of a project or a step you keep putting off. After entrusting it to God, take a first concrete step today.

**For reflection:** Which first concrete step have you been putting off for a long time?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 3.

## Day 98 — More than the fish

**Aim:** Remember that the goal is not only the result.

Before the breaking nets, Peter does not count the fish. He falls at Jesus' knees. Then they bring their boats to land and, as the Gospel says, "they forsook all, and followed him" (Luke 5:11). The book concludes that the goal of every professional blessing is "not to fill your nets, but to make you fall in love with the One who fills them." Today, thank God for Himself, not only for what you receive.

**For reflection:** What draws you to God, beyond what you receive?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 3.

## Day 99 — Faith and hands

**Aim:** Begin with what you have.

The book tells the story of Solange, a widow with two children, who wanted to sell doughnuts but was afraid to start with almost nothing. The author said to her: "Begin with what you have." Every morning she prayed before lighting the fire; every evening she gave thanks. She would say: "I had no capital. I had faith and hands." This story does not promise the same result to everyone. It invites you to look at what you already have in your hands.

**For reflection:** What do you already have in your hands to begin?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 3.

## Day 100 — Review the week: faith that acts

**Aim:** Take stock of faith and action.

This week spoke about the small door, a word that lifts someone up, honesty in the details, the first step you keep putting off, love for the One who fills the nets and faith that begins with little. Read over your records without comparing yourself to anyone. Notice one concrete step you took, even a modest one. The book sums it up in a few words: "Faith and hands."

**For reflection:** Which concrete step did you take this week?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 3; Habits & Challenges specification, section 9.

## Day 101 — Called at work

**Aim:** Be faithful where you are.

The book notes that God often calls people in the middle of their work. Elisha was ploughing, Ruth was gleaning, David was keeping the sheep, Peter was washing his nets. It draws a rule from this: "Faithfulness in the ordinary prepares promotion in the extraordinary — never the reverse." This is not a promise of quick promotion; it is an invitation to faithfulness in your present post. Today, do your ordinary work with care, because it matters.

**For reflection:** Where is God asking you to be faithful at the moment?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 3.

## Day 102 — Behind your counter

**Aim:** Work as for the Lord.

Paul writes: "And whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men" (Colossians 3:23). The book puts it this way: "it is Christ you serve behind your counter, your cash register, your steering wheel, your keyboard." This also applies to household tasks and to studies. Today, choose a task you usually do without thinking, and do it heartily, for the Lord.

**For reflection:** Which ordinary task do you want to do heartily today?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 3.

## Day 103 — Someone always checks

**Aim:** Stay honest even when it costs.

The book tells the story of Richard, an accountant in Manila, whom his colleagues found too scrupulous. He would answer: "Someone always checks. The One I serve sees everything." A promotion went to a more "flexible" colleague instead of him. He wrote his resignation, prayed, and left it in the drawer. The book says: "For two years, his faithfulness made him look like a loser." If honesty is costing you something at the moment, entrust that cost to God.

**For reflection:** Where is honesty costing you something at the moment?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 3.

## Day 104 — The envelope pushed back

**Aim:** Refuse a dishonest gain.

The author tells how, in the time of his debts, a man offered him an envelope to sign an arranged certificate. He admits that he did not say no straight away. Then he pushed the envelope back: "That evening, I came home with my debts intact — and my conscience intact." He adds: "that night, I slept like a rich man." If a doubtful offer comes your way, take time to pray and to seek counsel before you answer.

**For reflection:** In which situation do you sometimes feel pressure to arrange things?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 3.

## Day 105 — The King who serves

**Aim:** Accept a humble task.

Chapter 3 ends by looking at Jesus. The night before His death, He takes a towel and washes His disciples' feet — "the lowest work of the household" (John 13). He says: "I am among you as he that serveth" (Luke 22:27). The book calls Him "a Servant-King." Today, do a humble task that no one will notice, thinking of Him.

**For reflection:** Which humble task could you do today without being seen?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 3.

## Day 106 — What takes God's place

**Aim:** Recognise what you put your trust in.

The book makes clear that Mammon is not only money: "It is everything that seeks to take God's place in your trust — a salary, a relationship, a diploma, a position, an insurance policy." These things are often good. The problem begins when they become what reassures you most. Today, simply ask yourself what you lean on when you are afraid, and talk to God about it.

**For reflection:** What do you lean on first when you are afraid?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 4.

## Day 107 — Review the week: serving where you are

**Aim:** Take stock of faithfulness at work.

This week spoke about faithfulness in the ordinary, work done heartily, honesty that costs, a gain refused, the King who serves and what can take God's place. Read over your records without judging yourself. Notice a task done with more heart, or a difficult moment of honesty. It is often these small acts that shape character.

**For reflection:** Which small act of faithfulness do you keep from this week?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapters 3 and 4; Habits & Challenges specification, section 9.

## Day 108 — A slow substitution

**Aim:** Notice what has gradually replaced prayer.

The book tells the story of Serge, a believing businessman, who prayed less because of meetings and read less because of files. After a serious loss, he said: "I don't even know anymore when I stopped truly praying." The book describes this movement: "without noise, without a date, without a visible rupture. Just a slow substitution." Today, without accusing yourself, look at what may gradually have taken the place of your prayer.

**For reflection:** What has taken a little of the place of your prayer?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 4.

## Day 109 — "Have need of nothing"

**Aim:** Keep your need of God in good times.

The church of Laodicea said: "I am rich, and increased with goods, and have need of nothing" (Revelation 3:17). The book sees a danger in this: abundance can put the need for God to sleep. It offers a simple question: "do my blessings bring me closer to God, or pull me further from Him?" Think of a good thing you received recently. Let it lead you to gratitude rather than to forgetfulness.

**For reflection:** How did you welcome your latest good news before God?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 4.

## Day 110 — When greed dresses up as virtue

**Aim:** Call things by their name, gently.

The book observes that greed never presents itself under its true name: it calls itself "prudence," "foresight" or "business sense." Paul, for his part, speaks of "covetousness, which is idolatry" (Colossians 3:5). The book suggests a first remedy: name it before God. Prudence remains a real quality; the question is whether it sometimes hides a fear or an attachment. Take a moment to talk to God about it honestly.

**For reflection:** Does your prudence sometimes hide a fear?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 4.

## Day 111 — Rejoice in someone else's success

**Aim:** Replace comparison with joy.

The book describes a common reaction when someone else succeeds: looking for the flaw, saying "he must have cheated somewhere." It writes that this is often "covetousness wearing a judge's robe." In the chapter's table, the Kingdom's response to a brother's success is sincere joy. Think of someone around you who is succeeding. Today, rejoice for them and, if you can, congratulate them.

**For reflection:** Whose success can you rejoice in today?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 4.

## Day 112 — Do not wait until you have more

**Aim:** Let generosity begin now, within your means.

The book quotes a common sentence: "I will give when I have more." It answers that the amount is not the problem — the heart is — and that a closed heart does not open as income rises. So generosity can begin within the measure of what you have today: a little money if you can, but also time, a service, a shared meal. Choose a generous act within your reach, without putting yourself in difficulty.

**For reflection:** Which generous act is within your reach today?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 4.

## Day 113 — The joy of Zacchaeus

**Aim:** Let the presence of Jesus open your hands.

Zacchaeus, rich by dubious means, welcomes Jesus into his home. Without anyone asking him, he says: "Behold, Lord, the half of my goods I give to the poor; and if I have taken any thing from any man by false accusation, I restore him fourfold." Jesus answers: "This day is salvation come to this house." The book notes that the presence of Jesus did what no rule could do. If you have wronged someone, even a little, think about how to make it right.

**For reflection:** Is there a wrong you would like to make right?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 4.

## Day 114 — Review the week: hands that open

**Aim:** Take stock of money and the heart.

This week spoke about what gradually replaces prayer, the need for God in abundance, greed in disguise, joy at the success of others, generosity within your means and the joy of Zacchaeus. Read over your records without judging yourself. Notice whether your hands have opened a little, in money, time or attention. A single step is enough to keep going.

**For reflection:** Where have your hands opened a little this week?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 4; Habits & Challenges specification, section 9.

## Day 115 — Look your debts in the face

**Aim:** Take a first step of courage with your debts.

The book quotes Proverbs 22:7: "the borrower is servant to the lender." The author, who carried debts himself, suggests a first step: "take a complete inventory," in black and white. He admits that it hurts: "you do not heal a disease you refuse to diagnose." If you have debts, start by listing them on a piece of paper that stays yours. SÉNEVÉ asks for neither your amounts nor your creditors.

**For reflection:** Which debt, small or large, would you like to look in the face?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 4.

## Day 116 — One victory at a time

**Aim:** Move step by step towards freedom.

The book describes the path the author followed for three years: cutting back on the superfluous for a time, paying off the smallest debt first to create momentum, and refusing any new debt taken on to keep up appearances. It makes clear: "It is not the only possible method: it is the one I can testify to." If an important money decision is waiting for you, also seek counsel from a competent person. Today, choose a single small, realistic action.

**For reflection:** Which small, realistic action can you take this week?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 4.

## Day 117 — Behind the photos

**Aim:** Be set free from the need to appear.

The book describes our time: people "suffer in silence behind photos of happiness." It makes clear that luxury is not a sin, but that the need to appear can become a prison. It concludes: "Simplicity is not poverty. It is the freedom to stop playing a role." Today, spot an expense or a habit that mainly serves the image you give, and ask yourself whether you want to keep it.

**For reflection:** Which role would you like to stop playing?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 4.

## Day 118 — The King with open hands

**Aim:** Look at Jesus, who became poor.

Paul writes: "though he was rich, yet for your sakes he became poor, that ye through his poverty might be rich" (2 Corinthians 8:9). The book recalls Jesus "born in a borrowed manger," with nowhere to lay His head. It concludes that when Jesus asks you to open your hands, "He is not taking anything from you. He is teaching you to resemble Him." Take a moment to thank Him for what He gave.

**For reflection:** What does Jesus' choice to become poor stir in you?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 4.

## Day 119 — Put money back in its place

**Aim:** Make money a servant.

Chapter 4 ends like this: "Money is an excellent servant and a miserable god." Its prayer asks: "Keep me from becoming a slave of my own blessings." Today, before an expense, even a small one, take a few seconds to present it to God. This simple act reminds you who the Master is, and leaves money in its place: that of a tool.

**For reflection:** Which expense do you want to present to God today?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 4.

## Day 120 — A workshop where heaven touches the earth

**Aim:** Make a peaceful review of the month and choose one step.

Chapter 3 ends with this picture: "when a man works with Jesus, his workshop becomes a place where heaven touches the earth." This month spoke about work done with God, honesty, service and the place of money in the heart. Read over your weekly reviews and choose a single step for next month. Your review measures neither your faith nor your worth. Your daily habits continue, and you can adjust your times at any moment.

**For reflection:** Which step do you want to keep for next month?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 3.
