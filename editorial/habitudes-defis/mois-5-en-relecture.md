Month 5 returns in depth to two keys: Tzedakah, or helping the least (days 122 to 134, chapter 5), then faithfulness in the little things (days 136 to 148, chapter 6), with a review on day 150. Days 121, 128, 135, 142 and 149 review the week (one review every 7 days since day 37). Passages already used in month 2 are not repeated.

Sentences in quotation marks are quotations from the book or from the Bible; the rest is editorial adaptation to be validated. No text promises a miracle, healing, promotion or wealth, or measures a person's faith or worth. No amount is asked of you, and the help suggested always stays within each person's means. The questions invite reflection; they do not ask you to type anything.

## Day 121 — Review the week: walking towards freedom

**Aim:** Take stock of the week that opens the fifth month.

This week spoke about looking your debts in the face, moving forward one victory at a time, being set free from the need to appear, Jesus who became poor, money put back in its place and work done with God. Read over your records without judging yourself. Notice one step, even a small one, towards more freedom. A new month is beginning: it will speak about compassion and faithfulness.

**For reflection:** Which step towards more freedom did you take this week?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapters 3 and 4; Habits & Challenges specification, section 9.

## Day 122 — You do it unto Him

**Aim:** Recognise Jesus in the person in need.

In chapter 5, the book revisits the judgement scene in Matthew 25. The King does not ask how many services we attended. He asks: "Was I hungry — did you feed Me?" Then He says: "Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me" (Matthew 25:40). The book concludes: "The widow in your neighborhood — that is Him." Today, look around you with those eyes.

**For reflection:** Who around you needs to be seen today?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 5.

## Day 123 — A distress He knows

**Aim:** Approach the suffering of others with the eyes of Jesus.

The book notes that the King's questions describe His own life. He was hungry in the wilderness. As a child, He fled into Egypt as a refugee. He was stripped at Calvary and arrested at night. The book writes that He recognises His own "by a distress He Himself has inhabited." Think of someone living one of these situations: hunger, exile, illness, prison. Pray for them today, and look for a simple act to support them.

**For reflection:** Which distress close to you would you like to carry in prayer?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 5.

## Day 124 — The corner of the field

**Aim:** Plan a portion for others.

The book explains that the Hebrew word Tzedakah means justice. The law of Israel gave an image of it: the corner of the field was left unharvested, and clusters were left for the gleaner (Leviticus 19:9-10). It sums it up: "The field belongs to you; the corner of the field belongs to the poor." This corner can be a little money, a little time or a little food. Today, think about the corner you could set aside for others, within your means.

**For reflection:** Which corner of your field could you set aside for others?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 5.

## Day 125 — A pure heart and open hands

**Aim:** Hold holiness and compassion together.

James writes that pure religion is "To visit the fatherless and widows in their affliction, and to keep himself unspotted from the world" (James 1:27). The book points out that there are two dimensions, and that we often separate what God has joined. It concludes: "True religion holds both together: a pure heart and open hands." Today, notice which of the two asks for more of your attention at the moment.

**For reflection:** Between a pure heart and open hands, which one asks for your attention?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 5.

## Day 126 — A thousand francs a week

**Aim:** Begin Tzedakah with what you have.

The book tells the story of Nadège, a young woman on a very modest salary, who wanted to help Mama Joséphine, a widow with four children. She set aside "a thousand Congolese francs a week." Several months later, the children had gone back to school. The book concludes: "It begins with what you have, where you are, this week." This story sets no amount. Think of a small but regular help you could offer.

**For reflection:** Which small but regular help could you offer?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 5.

## Day 127 — What the world cannot argue with

**Aim:** Let love go together with faith.

The apostle John asks a question: "But whoso hath this world's good, and seeth his brother have need, and shutteth up his bowels of compassion from him, how dwelleth the love of God in him?" (1 John 3:17). The book notes that the deepest doctrine cannot live alongside a closed heart. It adds: "the world can argue with your doctrines — but it cannot argue with your love." Today, let your faith be seen through a concrete act of love.

**For reflection:** What concrete act of love can you do today?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 5.

## Day 128 — Review the week: recognising the King

**Aim:** Take stock of compassion.

This week spoke about the King hidden in the least, a distress He knows, the corner of the field set aside for others, a pure heart and open hands, a modest and regular help, and a love no one can argue with. Read over your records without judging yourself. Notice one person you saw more clearly this week. This is often how compassion begins: with a look.

**For reflection:** Which person did you see more clearly this week?

## Day 129 — Give and forget

**Aim:** Help without keeping a record.

The book observes that today, "Even charity has become content": people film the banknote they give, photograph the bag of rice handed to the widow. It recalls Jesus' word about giving in secret (Matthew 6:3-4), and adds: "True love gives and forgets. It keeps no register of its good deeds." Not exposing someone in their distress is also a way of protecting their dignity. Today, if you help someone, do it without telling anyone.

**For reflection:** How can you protect the dignity of the person you help?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 5.

## Day 130 — Teach someone your trade

**Aim:** Give a skill.

The book makes clear that Tzedakah is not only about money: "Skills are a currency." It writes that the untrained young man "needs you to teach him your trade." A shared skill can last longer than a gift. Think about what you know how to do: read, count, cook, repair, write a CV, use a phone. Today, offer to teach someone something useful.

**For reflection:** Which skill could you pass on to someone?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 5.

## Day 131 — Love that organises itself

**Aim:** Move from emotion to a lasting commitment.

The book notes that you can give once on a wave of emotion, then move on to something else. In Acts 6, when widows were being neglected, the apostles organised the help with "seven men full of the Spirit." The book concludes: "Emotion passes. Organized love remains." Today, choose a form of help you can keep up over time, even a modest one, on your own or with your church.

**For reflection:** Which modest help could you keep up over time?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 5.

## Day 132 — Moved with compassion

**Aim:** Notice the one who asks for nothing.

At the gates of Nain, a procession comes out to bury "the only son of his mother, and she was a widow" (Luke 7). The book points out that no one asks Jesus for anything. Yet when He saw her, "he had compassion on her." He stops, comes close and acts. Some people suffer without asking for anything. Today, ask God to help you notice someone who carries a sorrow in silence.

**For reflection:** Who around you may be carrying a sorrow in silence?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 5.

## Day 133 — Behold thy mother

**Aim:** Make room for someone who is alone.

On the cross, Jesus sees His mother and the disciple He loves. He says: "Woman, behold thy son." Then: "Behold thy mother" (John 19:26-27). The book notes: "As He is dying, the King places a widow in a family." A person who is alone often needs a place more than a gift: an invitation, a meal, a regular visit. Think of someone you could include more in your life or in your family.

**For reflection:** Which person who is alone could you invite more into your life?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 5.

## Day 134 — The needs we no longer see

**Aim:** Look at those around you with fresh eyes.

The prayer in chapter 5 asks: "Open my eyes to the needs I no longer see out of habit." The chapter ends like this: "You were seeking His face in your prayers. He was waiting for you in the eyes of the widow next door." Today, on your usual way or in your street, look as if it were the first time. Keep in your heart a need you no longer noticed.

**For reflection:** Which need had you stopped noticing out of habit?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 5.

This week spoke about the gift you forget, a skill to pass on, organised love, Jesus moved before the widow of Nain, a place made for someone who is alone and the needs we no longer see. Read over your records without comparing yourself to anyone. Notice one act you could repeat. The most useful compassion is often the kind that comes back, week after week.

## Day 136 — God looks at your habits

**Aim:** See faithfulness as an everyday character.

Chapter 6 begins with a sentence that echoes this journey: "God does not examine your dreams. He examines your habits." The book makes clear that faithfulness is not first a matter of money: "Arriving on time. Keeping your word. Finishing the work you began." It also mentions returning the borrowed tool and "Stacking the chairs after the service." Today, choose one of these small acts and do it with care.

**For reflection:** Which small act of faithfulness do you want to take care of today?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 6.

## Day 137 — The same words for each one

**Aim:** Stop comparing yourself.

In the parable of the talents, the servant who received five talents and the one who received two hear exactly the same words: "Well done, thou good and faithful servant." The book concludes that God "measures the faithfulness in the managing," not the quantity managed. So you are not in competition with the one who earns more than you. Today, thank God for what He has entrusted to you, without comparing it with what others have received.

**For reflection:** Who do you tend to compare yourself with?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 6.

## Day 138 — What we bury out of fear

**Aim:** Dare to use what you have received.

The third servant in the parable buried his talent out of fear. The book points out that he "was not condemned for having received little, but for having done nothing with what he had received." A gift, a skill, an idea or a small sum can stay buried for a long time. Today, name one thing you keep hidden out of fear, and ask God for the courage to put it to a first use.

**For reflection:** What have you buried out of fear?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 6.

## Day 139 — Fifty dollars as if it were a million

**Aim:** Manage little with care.

The book tells the story of Dieu-Merci, who sold peanuts in front of a school, starting with fifty dollars. Every evening he wrote down his takings and expenses in a school exercise book. His friends laughed at him. He later said: "I learned to manage fifty dollars as if it were a million." The book adds that promotion "revealed what he already was." Today, notice how carefully you manage the little you have.

**For reflection:** Which small area would you like to manage with more care?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 6.

## Day 140 — Faithful in prison

**Aim:** Stay faithful when faithfulness does not pay.

Joseph was faithful in Potiphar's house, and he was thrown into prison on a false accusation. The book acknowledges it: "faithfulness does not always pay right away." Yet in prison, Joseph manages the dungeon as he managed the house (Genesis 39:21-22). The book writes: "His faithfulness did not depend on his wages — it depended on his God." If you are going through an injustice, entrust it to God and keep one faithful act today.

**For reflection:** Where does your faithfulness seem to bring nothing at the moment?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 6.

## Day 141 — The day of small beginnings

**Aim:** Do not despise your beginning.

The prophet Zechariah writes: "For who hath despised the day of small things?" (Zechariah 4:10). The book recalls that Noah, Moses and David went through long, hidden years before their mission. It advises: "do not compare your Chapter 1 with someone else's Chapter 20." The habits of this journey may also be small beginnings. Today, water your seed without comparing yourself.

**For reflection:** Which small beginning do you tend to despise?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 6.

This week spoke about the habits God looks at, comparison, what we bury out of fear, little managed with care, faithfulness in injustice and small beginnings. ## Day 143 — The logbook has two readers

**Aim:** Be faithful in what no one checks.

The book tells the story of Emmanuel, a night watchman. His colleagues did one round in two and filled in the logbook in advance. He did every round and wrote down the exact hour. He explained: "The logbook has two readers, Brother Justin. My boss, who doesn't read it. And my God, who always reads it." Think of a task in your life that no one really checks. Today, do it completely, for God.

**For reflection:** Which task that no one checks do you want to do completely?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 6.

## Day 144 — Disorder is not faith

**Aim:** Put a little order into your finances.

The book answers those who say a budget is not spiritual: "Disorder is not faith." It proposes an order of priorities: God's portion and help for the poor, essential needs, savings, debt repayment, then wants and leisure. It notes that many live this order upside down. If you wish, write your own order today on a piece of paper that stays yours. SÉNEVÉ does not ask you for any figure.

**For reflection:** In what order does your money really go out?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 6.

## Day 145 — Management that speaks

**Aim:** Honour your small money commitments.

The book notes that Potiphar, the keeper of the prison and Pharaoh saw God in Joseph's management without hearing him preach. Pharaoh said: "Can we find such a one as this is, a man in whom the Spirit of God is?" (Genesis 41:38). The book adds: "Your colleagues watch whether you repay the small sums you borrow — or whether you 'forget.'" Today, settle a small debt or keep a commitment that is still waiting.

**For reflection:** Which small commitment is still waiting to be kept?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 6.

## Day 146 — The serenity that raises questions

**Aim:** Let your trust open conversations.

The book tells the story of a medical student in Chennai, with a very small income, who gave regularly and with joy. Her teacher asked her: "How do you manage to be so serene when you have almost nothing?" She answered: "I give to God first. And I trust Him for the rest." This simple answer opened a long conversation. Your peace, too, can speak to those around you.

**For reflection:** If someone asked you where your peace comes from, what would you answer?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 6.

## Day 147 — Under the fig tree

**Aim:** Know that God sees you in hidden faithfulness.

Jesus says to Nathanael: "when thou wast under the fig tree, I saw thee" (John 1:48). The book explains that the fig tree here is "the private, invisible place where no one is watching." It writes: "Nothing of your invisible faithfulness has escaped His eyes." The exact change given back, the notebook kept in the evening, the task done with care: God sees them. Today, do one faithful act that only God will see.

**For reflection:** Which hidden faithfulness would you like to offer God today?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 6.

## Day 148 — Enter into the joy

**Aim:** Remember the true goal of faithfulness.

The parable of the talents ends with these words: "enter thou into the joy of thy lord." The book points out that the final reward is not an amount: "It is His joy — it is Him." It adds: "Faithfulness in the little things is not a promotion technique: it is a long walk toward that face." Today, live your habits as a path towards Him, and not as a performance.

**For reflection:** What changes if your habits are a path towards God?

Working source: Stop Begging for Miracles, English edition provided, chapter 6.

**Aim:** Make a peaceful review of the month and choose one step.
